# Joke candidates

Scores are 1-5 in this order: office fit and laugh, truthful observation, surprise, tightness, originality. They are comparative judgments, not claims of objective quality.

| 7 | The project plan lists yesterday as a milestone; the deadline moved up twice. | 4 | 4 | 3 | 4 | 3 | 18 |

Candidate 3 wins because the phrase is recognizably office-email language and the subject line literally moves the implied threat ahead of its explanation. Candidate 1 has a strong bureaucratic reversal, but the timing can read as a continuity error; candidate 2 observes standups well but lands closer to an amusing summary than a punchline. Candidate 4 has the clearest consequence, though its automation-versus-manager comparison is familiar. Candidate 5 has a real reorg observation but a softer comic turn. Candidate 6's locked spreadsheet is a crisp contradiction, but less surprising. Candidate 7 is legible and compact but does not turn sharply enough.

Picked candidate 3. Cut "line" and replaced "so ... before the message" with the shorter closing beat "; the threat arrives first." Final entry: I put 'per my last email' in the subject; the threat arrives first.
